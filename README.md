# EZ Composer

A native macOS music app written in C++ with [JUCE](https://juce.com). It used to be called Anthropocene Music.

It has as many instruments as you like, two to start with, each on its own grand staff and played by its own VST3 plugin, that you can write notes and chords for, and an 88-key piano. Chords are defined the way the Chord Progression Builder defines them, one measure at a time. A score can have several pages, each with its own key, time signature and music, played one after another or one at a time. Sound goes to the Mac's default audio output.

## Building

You need macOS 12 or later, the Xcode Command Line Tools, and CMake 3.22 or later (`brew install cmake`).

```bash
cmake -B build -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Debug
cmake --build build -j
open "build/EZComposer_artefacts/Debug/EZ Composer.app"
```

The first configure downloads JUCE 9.0.2 into `build/`.

Saving playback as an MP3 (see **MP3** under [Toolbar](#toolbar)) uses [LAME](https://lame.sourceforge.io), which the app runs to encode it: install it with `brew install lame`. Everything else works without it.

## Using it

The window has a toolbar along the top, the score's settings on the left, the instruments' staves, one above the other, and the piano under them.

Every control has a short tip, two lines at most, saying what it does and how to use it, which shows when the pointer rests on it for a moment: the buttons, menus and boxes in the toolbar, the sidebar and over the staves, the volume dials, the page tabs, the ruler and the piano, and those in the chord window, the scale's settings and the effects page. The annotation buttons' tips say what each mark means as well, such as **mf**'s *fairly loud*, and the velocity its notes play at. A greyed-out control's tip says why it can't be used yet.

### Instruments

The score has two instruments to start with, and can have any number. They share the key, time signature, tempo and measures, and play together in time, but each has its own notes, chords, dynamics, alternate staff and plugin. Instrument 1's staves are at the top and the others' below, in order; their measures line up, spaced to fit what's in any of them. Each instrument's name and plugin are written at its top left, e.g. "Instrument 1 · Pigments".

**Adding and deleting instruments.** The **+** below the lowest instrument's staves, at the left, adds one below it, with its own staves, volume dial and plugin. It starts empty, without a plugin, at 0 dB, becomes the active instrument, and is scrolled into view. With more instruments than fit, scroll up and down through them with a trackpad, the mouse wheel or the scroll bar; the volume dials scroll with them. To the right of each instrument's staves, past the end of the score and level with their middle, is a **bin** button that deletes the instrument, with its staves. The bins are in line with the measure buttons (**+**, **−** and **Clone**) and scroll with the staves, and the measure buttons move up or down just enough to stay clear of them. An instrument with nothing in it and no plugin goes straight away. Otherwise you're asked first, with **Delete** or **Cancel**, since its notes, chords and dynamics go with it and its plugin is unloaded. The instruments after it move up, with their music, plugins and volumes, and are numbered again. The last instrument can't be deleted. Adding and deleting instruments can be undone; undoing a delete puts the music back in its place, but the plugin needs loading again. New starts again with two instruments, the first two keeping their plugins; opening a score gives it as many instruments as it was saved with.

Just after the final barline, halfway down the staves in view, **+** and **−**, side by side, add a measure at the end and take the last one away, for every instrument; **−** is greyed out unless the last measure shows nothing but rests in every instrument (no notes and no chord, though dynamics and hairpins don't count), and when there's only one measure; its tooltip says why. **Clone**, under them, repeats all the measures after the last one, for every instrument: 4 measures become 8, the second 4 a copy of the first, with all their notes and chords, to be edited on their own. They scroll sideways with the staves, so they're in view when the end of the score is, but stay halfway down what's in view as you scroll up and down through the instruments. The staves reach all the way to the right edge of the window. Adding or cloning measures scrolls to the end, so the new measures are in view. Scroll sideways with a trackpad, the mouse wheel, or the scroll bar. Each instrument's staves take only the height their music needs: the chord buttons sit just over the chord names, which sit just over the highest notes, and the space above and below grows only when notes reach far beyond the staves.

One instrument is **active**, marked by a blue bar beside its staves. The piano and MIDI controllers play the active instrument, and the toolbar's plugin controls, the sidebar's **Alternate staff** and **Copy Chords** are the active instrument's. Choose it from the instrument menu in the toolbar, or click anywhere on an instrument's track: in its staves, whatever the click does there, on its chord buttons, past the end of the score to the right of its staves, or to the left of them, beside its volume dial (clicking the dial itself mutes it, and doesn't make it active). Above the first instrument's staves counts as the first's, and below the last's as the last's. Keys held down when another instrument becomes active are let go; the score's notes play on.

Each instrument has a **volume dial** to the left of its staves, setting how loud it is in the mix, from **Off** up to +6 dB. It starts at 0 dB, which leaves the plugin as loud as it is. Drag up or down, or round, to turn it. **Click** it without turning it to mute the instrument, and click again to unmute it; a muted dial is grey and says **Muted**, and unmuting brings back the volume it had. Turning a muted dial unmutes it. The dials follow the staves as they scroll up and down. Volumes change smoothly, without clicks, even while the score plays. A volume change, or muting, counts as a change to the score, which saves each instrument's volume and whether it's muted; they're also remembered the next time the app starts, and stay as they are for a new score.

### Toolbar

- **Play**, the green arrow (or the space bar, which pauses it while it plays), plays from the beat chosen on the ruler of the page you're on (see below), its beginning to start with: with **Piece**, on through every page after it, and with **Page**, just that page (see [Pages](#pages)). It highlights each beat, following it along the staff. While it plays, it becomes **Stop**, a black square, which stops it, recording too, and goes back to the beginning: of the piece with **Piece**, showing the first page, and of the page with **Page**. Every page plays from its start again, as it does when the score plays to the end by itself. Changes to the score while it plays are heard as soon as playback reaches them: a note added ahead of the beat that's playing plays when it gets there (one added behind it, or under it, waits for the next time through a loop, rather than starting part way). A note that's sounding stops at once if it's taken out, or at its new end if it's made shorter, and a pedal marked down or taken out where it's playing goes down or comes up straight away.
- **Pause**, two bars beside Play (or the space bar while the score plays), stops the score where it's got to, recording too, and leaves the ruler's marker there, on the page it was playing, which is shown if it wasn't: Play, or Record, carries on from there, the same time through a repeat as it was. Choosing another place to play from first, on the ruler or with the buttons below, or going to another page, plays from there instead. It's greyed out while nothing's playing.
- **Record**, the red circle beside Pause, plays the score as Play does, from the same beat, and records what you play on the piano or a MIDI controller into the active instrument (see [Recording](#recording)). It's tinted red while recording. Pressed while the score's already playing, it starts recording from there; pressed while recording, it stops recording and the score plays on.
- **Back to Start**, **Rewind** and **Fast Forward**, beside Record and drawn as a bar with an arrow pointing back to it, two arrows pointing back, and two pointing on, move through the score, whether it's playing or stopped. **Back to Start** goes to the start of the page you're on. **Rewind** goes back to the start of the measure before the one it's at, and **Fast Forward** on to the start of the next. Stopped, they move where the page plays from, which the ruler's marker shows, a measure at a time; the staves scroll to keep it in view, and Play and Record start from there. With **Piece**, Fast Forward from the page's last measure goes on to the start of the next page, and Rewind from its first measure back to the last measure of the page before; with **Page**, they stay on the page. Playing, they jump straight there, through the measures as they're played, repeats and all, carrying on from there, recording too; with **Piece**, on into the next page or back into the one before, the staves following along. With **Loop** on, Fast Forward in the last measure goes round to the start, and Rewind in the first measure round to the last; without it, Fast Forward in the last measure does nothing, and Rewind in the first goes back to its start. Paused, they move on from where it's paused.
- **Loop**, beside Fast Forward and drawn as two arrows going round in a loop, the upper one to the right and the lower one back to the left, makes the piece, or the page, play over and over, going straight from the end back to the beginning, the start of the first page for the piece, until you press **Stop**. It can be turned on or off while the score plays; turning it off lets the score finish this time through. It's blue while it's on, and is remembered the next time the app starts.
- **Piece** or **Page**, beside Loop, says what Play, Record and Loop play: **Piece**, every page, one after another, or **Page**, just the page you're on. Click it to change it, even while the score plays: it carries on from where it is if it can. It's remembered the next time the app starts.
- **MP3**, beside Piece or Page, records what's heard as the score plays, to save as an MP3. Turn it on, and Play, or Record, writes everything you hear from the speakers, the instruments with their effects and anything played on the piano or a MIDI controller as it plays, but not the metronome; you still hear it all as usual. Paused, nothing's written, and it carries on when the score plays again, so the pauses aren't in it. When the score stops, by Stop or at the end, it goes on for a moment while the sound dies away, up to five seconds, and then asks where to save the MP3, named after the score and beside it if it's been saved, or in Music. Cancel throws the recording away. The MP3 is made in the background, a variable bit rate one of high quality (LAME's -V 2, around 190 kbps), titled after the score, and you're told if it couldn't be made. Turning MP3 off while the score plays finishes the recording there. It's blue while it's on, and isn't remembered the next time the app starts. It needs LAME (see [Building](#building)): without it, turning it on says how to install it.
- The **metronome**, beside MP3 and drawn as one, ticks each beat while the score plays or records: a sharp metallic clang, like a cowbell's, on the first beat of each measure, and a woody click on the others, so a 3/4 measure goes clang, click, click. It's blue while it's on, can be turned on or off while the score plays (it's heard from the next beat), keeps time through a loop and with tempo changes, and is remembered the next time the app starts. It sounds through the speakers alongside the instruments, at a fixed level, and isn't sent to the instruments or saved with the score.
- **BPM** sets the tempo in quarter notes per minute, from 20 to 300, fractions allowed. The default is 120. Type it, or step it with the up and down arrows beside it, a beat per minute at a time (from a fraction, to the whole number either side); holding an arrow down goes on stepping, and is undone in one go. While typing in it, the up and down arrow keys step it too. An arrow is greyed out at the end of the range. A change while the score plays takes over straight away, from the beat it's got to.
- The instrument menu (**Instrument 1**, **Instrument 2** and so on) chooses the active instrument.
- **Load Instrument…** loads a VST3 instrument plugin for the active instrument. It opens a list of the instruments loaded lately, by any instrument, the latest first, each with its maker and plugin file (its tooltip says where the file is). Double-click one, or choose it and click **Load** or press Return, to load it straight away, with its own sound to start with. **Browse…** finds another plugin with a file browser, such as one in `/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3`, which then goes at the top of the list; a file with several instruments in it offers a menu of them. **Remove** takes the chosen one off the list without uninstalling it, and **Cancel** or Escape closes the list. The list keeps the last 12, and leaves out any whose plugin isn't there any more. Instruments loaded by opening a score go on it too; the ones reloaded when the app starts go at the end if they aren't already on it, so it isn't empty the first time. **Edit [plugin]**, named for the loaded plugin (e.g. **Edit Pigments**), opens the plugin's own window, where you can choose its sounds. The instruments can use different plugins, or the same one with different sounds.
- **Effects**, at the right of the toolbar, opens the effects page, for playing the instruments through VST3 effect plugins (see [Effects](#effects)).
- Each instrument's plugin is saved with the score (see [Saving and opening](#saving-and-opening)). It's also loaded again the next time the app starts, with the sound it had. It's saved when you load it, when you close its editor, and when you quit, in `~/Library/Application Support/EZ Composer`. If it can't be loaded, say because it's been uninstalled, the app tells you and forgets it.

### Pages

A score can have several pages. Each page has its own key, major or minor, time signature, alternate staffs, measures, repeats and music: everything in the sidebar, and everything on its staves. The instruments, with their plugins, volumes and effects, and the tempo are the score's, the same on every page, so every page has the same instruments' staves; adding or deleting an instrument does so on every page, and deleting one with music on any page asks first.

- Tabs along the top of the staves, **Page 1**, **Page 2** and so on, or the pages' names, show the pages; the one you're editing is joined to the ruler under it. Click a tab to go to its page: the staves, the ruler and the sidebar show that page, from its first measure.
- **+**, after the tabs, adds a page after the last and goes to it. A new page has blank staves for every instrument, four empty measures, in the key and time signature, and with the alternate staffs, of the page you were on, to change as you like.
- Drag a tab along the row to move its page to another place among them: the tab follows the mouse, the others move aside to show where it'll go, and letting go moves the page there and goes to it. Tabs without names are numbered by place, so the page takes its new number, keeping its music, settings and where it plays from; a named page keeps its name. With **Piece**, the pages play in their new order. A tab moved just a little is a click, and Undo puts the pages back in their old order.
- Right-click a tab for its menu. **Rename Page…** puts a box over the tab with the page's name in it, selected: type a new one, up to 32 characters, and press Return, or click anywhere else, to keep it, or press Esc to leave it as it was. A named page keeps its name wherever it's moved to, and the name's saved with the score, so it's there when the score's opened again; renaming is undone like any other change. Emptying the box, or typing the page's number, as **Page 2**, takes the name away, so the tab shows the page's number again. The tabs are all as wide as the longest name needs, up to a point, and narrower when there are too many to fit; a name that doesn't fit ends with an ellipsis, and the tab's tip shows it whole.
- **Copy Page** puts a copy of the page just after it, with all its music, settings, repeats and endings, playing from where the page does, and goes to it; the copy is a page of its own, to change as you like, named after its page with *copy* after the name, as **Chorus copy**, if the page has a name. **Delete Page…** takes the page out, with its music, after asking, by its name if it has one, with **Delete** or **Cancel**; Undo puts it back. The only page can't be deleted.
- Measures are each page's own, so **+**, **−** and **Clone** after the final barline work on the page you're on, and so do **Copy Chords** and **Generate Notes from Key**.
- With **Piece**, Play plays from where the page you're on plays from, and on through the pages after it, each in its own time signature, with its own repeats and endings. As the music goes on to the next page, the page you're looking at goes with it, if it's the one that was playing; if you've gone to another page, it stays there. The metronome's clang is on each page's own downbeats. With **Loop** on, after the last page it goes back to the start of the first.
- With **Page**, Play plays just the page you're on, and Loop loops it. Going to another page while it plays plays that page instead, from where it plays from.
- Going to another page isn't a change to the score, and isn't undone.

### Effects

**Effects**, at the right of the toolbar, opens the effects page, in a window of its own. Effects go in **buses**: chains of effect plugins, through which instruments can be sent.

- **Instruments**, at the top, lists each instrument, with where it goes: **Main output**, straight to the speakers, as before there were effects, or a bus. Each instrument goes to one place, and any number can go to the same bus.
- **Buses** lists the buses, each as a card. **Add Bus** adds one; its bin takes it out, with its effects, and whatever went to it goes to the main output instead.
- Each bus's effects are shown in order, joined by arrows, as the sound goes through them: what comes out of one goes into the next. **Add Effect…** loads a VST3 effect, such as one from `/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3`, at the end of the chain; a file with several effects in it offers a menu of them, and an instrument plugin isn't an effect, so it isn't loaded. On each effect, **Edit** opens the plugin's own window, to change its settings; **Bypass** passes over it, as if it weren't there, while keeping its place and settings, and the effect is greyed out while it's bypassed; the triangles move it earlier or later in the chain; and the cross takes it out.
- Each bus **goes to** the main output, or on to a bus after it, so buses can be chained: an instrument can go through one bus's effects and then another's. A bus can only go to one after it, so nothing goes round in a loop.

An instrument's volume and muting apply before its effects. The effects play all the time, whether or not the score is playing, so a reverb or delay rings on after Stop, and they hear the score's tempo, for effects that keep time with it. The metronome goes straight to the main output, not through the effects.

The effects, their settings and where everything goes are saved with the score, and kept for the next time the app starts, as the instruments are. Changing them, or an effect's settings in its window, counts as a change to the score that needs saving, though not one Undo undoes. Instruments added or taken out keep where they go. An effect that can't be loaded, say because it's been uninstalled, is left out, and the app says which.

### Saving and opening

The **File** menu saves and opens scores:

| Command | Shortcut | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **New** | ⌘N | Starts an empty score: four measures of 4/4 in C major at 120 BPM. |
| **Open…** | ⌘O | Opens a saved score. |
| **Open Recent** | | Lists the 10 scores opened or saved most recently, newest first, and opens the one you choose. Scores that have been moved or deleted aren't listed, and two with the same name say which folder they're in. **Clear Menu** empties the list. The list is remembered from one run of the app to the next, and the scores also appear in the Mac's own recent documents. |
| **Save** | ⌘S | Saves the score to its file, asking for a name the first time. |
| **Save As…** | ⇧⌘S | Saves the score to a new file, which it then keeps using. |

- Scores are saved as `.ezscore` files, which are JSON. The `.amscore` files saved when the app was called Anthropocene Music open just the same, and Open lists both. Saving one again, with Save or when asked before New, Open or quitting, moves it to `.ezscore`: it's saved as a file of the same name with the new extension, in the same folder, and the old file goes to the Trash, where it can still be got back; the recent scores list the new file instead. If there's already an `.ezscore` file of that name, it's saved in its own file instead, rather than over the other. Save As to another file saves it there, as `.ezscore` unless you give it `.amscore` on purpose. A file keeps everything about the score, and the plugins it's played on and their volumes: the key, time signature, tempo, and for each instrument its alternate staff, its measures and their notes, and each measure's chord with all its settings. That includes notes you've added to or taken out of a chord, a chord's notes moved with Edit, and the order a Random chord plays in. Notes on beats hidden by a shorter time signature are kept too.
- A score with several pages saves them all: the first page as a score on its own is saved, with the other pages after it, so an app from before there were pages opens just the first page. Opening a score goes to its first page.
- The effects are saved with the score too, with their settings, the buses they're in, and where each instrument and bus goes (see [Effects](#effects)). Opening a score saved with effects puts them in place of the ones you had; one saved without them leaves yours as they are.
- Each instrument's plugin is saved with the score, with its sound: whatever you've chosen in its editor. Opening the score loads the plugins again, or just gives it the saved sound if it's the one already loaded. A plugin's sound can be large, so a score can be too; Pigments adds about half a megabyte.
- Loading a different instrument, or changing its sound in its editor, counts as a change that needs saving, and the title shows it within half a second. It goes by what the instrument reports: a change to one of its parameters, its program, or its other settings. Just opening the editor, or playing, isn't a change, even though some instruments (Pigments, for one) save details of their editor window along with their sound.
- If a score's plugin can't be loaded, say because it isn't installed, the score still opens, you're told, and the plugin you had stays loaded. An instrument saved without a plugin also leaves the one it has as it is, and so does New. Scores saved before there were two instruments open as instrument 1, with their plugin, and an empty instrument 2.
- The window's title shows the score's name, and "Edited" when it has changes that haven't been saved. Before New, Open, or quitting (including closing the window), you're asked whether to save them.
- If a file can't be opened, because it isn't a score, you're told why and the current score stays as it was. A score with values out of range, say from editing the file by hand, opens with those values set back to their defaults.
- A newly opened or new score starts scrolled to the beginning, with the chord window closed.

### Saved progressions and rhythms

Progressions saved with **Save Progression…** and rhythms saved with **Save Rhythm…** aren't kept in scores. They're kept in the app's settings file, so every score can use them:

```
~/Library/Application Support/EZ Composer/EZ Composer.settings
```

The first time EZ Composer starts, if that file isn't there yet, it starts with a copy of the settings it had when it was called Anthropocene Music, from `~/Library/Application Support/Anthropocene Music/Anthropocene Music.settings`, with the instrument, effects, recent scores, and saved progressions and rhythms. The old file stays where it was.

It's the file that also keeps the instrument, the effects, the recent scores, the recent instruments (`recentInstruments`, as XML), and the toolbar's Loop, metronome and Piece or Page. It's XML, with a value for each setting, and these hold the saved progressions and rhythms, each as JSON:

| Setting | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `savedProgressions` | Each saved progression: its name, the key it was saved in, and its chords, by their numerals, added notes, inversions and octaves, with any notes set on the piano |
| `savedRhythms` | Each saved rhythm: its name, the time signature and number of measures it was saved from, and the treble and bass staves' notes, each by when it starts and how long it is, in 32nds, whether it's staccato, and whether it's tied to the next |
| `progressionChordType`, `progressionClef` | The chord type and clef a progression was last applied with, which Apply Saved Progression starts with |

Each is there once something's been saved or applied. They're written as soon as one is saved or deleted.

- Copying the file to another Mac takes the progressions and rhythms with it, along with the other settings.
- Deleting it loses them, with the other settings.
- Sharing a score doesn't share them, as they aren't in it.

### Ruler

Along the top of the staves, under the pages' tabs and above the note length and dynamic buttons, is a ruler for the page you're on, lined up with its measures and scrolling sideways with them: a tall tick and the number for each measure, on its first beat, and short ticks for its other beats. A marker shows where the page plays from, as an outline while it's stopped, and moves along filled in while it plays or records; while another page is playing, there's no marker. Each page has its own place to play from.

Click the ruler to choose the beat nearest the click as where Play and Record start, or drag along it. While the score plays, clicking or dragging jumps there straight away, and it carries on from there; the notes sounding stop, the pedal goes down or comes up as it is there, and notes that started before it aren't started part way. While recording, keys held as it jumps end where it jumped from. Stopping takes the marker back to the beginning, and pausing leaves it where it paused. It stays within the score as measures are taken away, and a new or opened score starts from the beginning. With **Loop** on, the score plays on from there to the end, and then loops from the very beginning.

### Undo

The **Edit** menu's **Undo** (⌘Z) undoes the last change to the score, and pressing it again undoes the one before, back to how the score was when it was opened or started (the last 500 changes are remembered). **Redo** (⇧⌘Z) makes an undone change again, until something else is changed. Each is greyed out when there's nothing to undo or redo.

- Undo covers every page, and adding, copying, moving and deleting pages. Undoing or redoing a change on another page goes to that page, to show it.
- Everything about the score can be undone: notes, ties, everything one recording added, chords and the chord window's changes, dynamics and hairpins, the key, time signature, tempo and alternate staff, and adding, removing, cloning and copying measures and progressions. What one click, drag or command does is undone in one go, e.g. a whole Copy Chords, a hairpin however far it was dragged, or everything an eraser stroke took out.
- The chord window shows the chord as undoing leaves it, and closes if its measure's undone away.
- Undoing counts as a change that needs saving. The instruments, their volumes and muting, and what's chosen to add, such as the note length, aren't part of what's undone.
- Typing in the BPM box, ⌘Z undoes the typing instead.

### Score

These are the settings of the page you're on; each page has its own (see [Pages](#pages)).

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Key** | The key's tonic, just its letter, with **Major** and **Minor** beside it: the 15 major keys, C♭ to C♯, and the 15 minor keys, A♭ to A♯. A minor key has the key signature of the major key a minor third above, its relative major, and its scale is the natural minor, the notes of the signature. **Minor** and **Major** keep the tonic, so C major becomes C minor, with three flats; a tonic without a key in the other mode takes the one that sounds the same, so G♭ major becomes F♯ minor. The key sets the key signature, the sharps and flats of notes you click in, and the scale **Generate Notes from Key** adds notes from. The chord window's numerals count up the key's own scale from its tonic, the natural minor in a minor key: in A minor, i is A minor, III is C, v is E minor and V7 is E7, with its G♯. Changing between major and minor, chords keep their numerals, and chords set on the piano keep their notes and are named again in the new key. Chords follow their numerals into a new key; notes keep their pitches and get accidentals if they need them. The key is saved with the score, minor or major. |
| **Time signature** | 2/4, 3/4 or 4/4. Notes on beats that no longer fit are kept, and come back if you go back to more beats. |
| **Alternate staff** | For the active instrument, what every chord puts on the other staff: **None**, the chord's **Root**, the root in an **Octave**, or the whole chord as a **Block chord** or **Rolled chord**, held for the measure. A **Root** or **Octave** under a chord is tied over to the next measure when that has the same chord, by its numeral (or, without one, the same notes set on the piano), however each is written, so it's held on under both rather than played again: under two measures of IVM7, one Random and one arpeggio, the root is held for both. A Block chord or Rolled chord on the alternate staff is played again in each measure, unless the chord itself is tied over. It applies to all the instrument's chords, on whichever staff each one isn't; each instrument has its own. Quarter notes under or over a chord are hidden while there's an alternate staff, rather than lost, and come back with None. Changing it puts any alternate-staff notes you've changed by hand back to what the chords give. Scores saved before this was one setting open with the alternate staff most of their chords had. |
| **Progression** | **Copy Chords to Instrument 2** (or **to Instrument 1**, when instrument 2 is active) gives the other instrument the active instrument's chords. With more than two instruments it's **Copy Chords To…**, which asks which instrument to copy to; with only one, there's nowhere to copy to, and it's disabled. The progression goes over measure by measure, in the same clefs, chord types and Random orders. It replaces what the other instrument had: its chords, and its quarter notes, hidden ones included; measures without a chord come over empty. The other instrument keeps its own alternate staff. If the other instrument has any notes or chords, you're asked first, with **Replace** or **Cancel**; if it's empty, the progression is copied straight away. The button is disabled while the active instrument has no chords. |
| **Save Progression…** | Saves the active instrument's chord progression, to apply again in this score or any other. ### Notes

Clicking the staff adds a note at that pitch and beat, taking its sharp or flat from the key signature. A blue note shows where it will go. Clicking between the staves puts the note on the nearer staff, with ledger lines. Rests fill the beats without notes.

Nine small buttons in the top left corner of the score, showing a whole, a dotted half, a half, a dotted quarter, a quarter, a dotted eighth, an eighth, a 16th and a 32nd note, choose the length of the notes that clicks add. They stay in the corner as the staves scroll. Quarter notes are chosen each time the app opens, and the blue note shows the length chosen. A note is as long as there's room for in its measure: a half note on the last beat of 4/4 is a quarter note, a dotted half on the third beat is a half note, a dotted quarter on the last beat is a quarter, and a whole note in 3/4 is a dotted half. A longer note takes the place of any notes on the beats it covers. A click while a note on the same staff is still sounding, after its own beat, doesn't cut it short: the new note goes where that one ends, the next point a note can start. So with quarter notes chosen, clicking on beat 2 after a dotted quarter on beat 1 puts the quarter halfway through beat 2, and the next click, on beat 3, puts the next one halfway through beat 3, building a dotted rhythm. A note that lasts to the barline sends it to the start of the next measure, and in the last measure there's nowhere for it to go; the faint note shows where a click will add it. A note can still be cut short from the Edit tool's menu, or by dragging another note into it. Notes starting on the same beat share a length, so a note added to them gives them its own. A shorter time signature shows notes shorter if it has to, and they're as long as they were again in 4/4. Notes shorter than a beat go on the nearest step of their own length without its dot: eighths and dotted eighths on the beats and halfway between them, 16ths on every 16th, and 32nds on every 32nd. Dotted notes are written with a dot in a space beside the notehead, past the flag of a flagged note with its stem up; a dotted eighth and a 16th that follows it share a beam. The other lengths go on the nearest beat, though a note already between beats can still be clicked to take it out. Four eighths in a row, each starting as the one before ends, share one beam (eight in a row are two groups of four); otherwise eighths, 16ths and 32nds are beamed together a beat at a time, with a second beam joining the 16ths and 32nds and a third the 32nds, and a short beam for one with no neighbour to share it. So three eighths in a row are a beamed pair and a flagged eighth, and a lone eighth, 16th or 32nd has its flags. Parts of beats left empty get the eighth, 16th and 32nd rests that fill them, in step with the beat. Notes play for as long as they're written, and their lengths are saved with the score; scores saved before there were note lengths open with quarter notes, and scores saved before eighth notes, or before 16ths and 32nds, open with their notes on the same beats as before.

Clicking a note takes it out again, whatever its sharp or flat. The note under the pointer turns red to show that a click will remove it. Notes are added and taken out when the mouse is let go, as long as it hasn't moved.

**Ties.** Drag from a note to the next note of the same pitch, the one that starts just as it ends, and let go: they're tied, with the usual curved tie between the noteheads, and play as one note held for both their lengths. A tie can cross a barline, so a note can last longer than a measure, and ties can be chained. A blue tie follows the mouse as you drag, and turns solid over a note it can tie to. Dragging between two tied notes again unties them. Letting go anywhere else, over a note of another pitch, over an empty spot, or over a same-pitched note that doesn't follow on, does nothing. A single note's tie curves away from its stem; in notes stacked on one beat, the upper ties curve up and the lower down. Taking out a tied note, or writing a longer note over it, takes its tie away too. Ties are saved with the score, and Clone copies them. A chord's notes don't tie.

**Staccato.** The button with a dot, just right of the 32nd note and a little apart from the lengths, makes the notes that clicks add staccato, whatever their length. It's blue while it's on, and stays on, for every note you add, until you click it again; it's off each time the app opens. Clicking it while marking a dynamic, hairpin, pedal or repeat, or with the eraser, goes back to adding notes, and the faint note that shows where a click will add one has the dot too. Notes added from the scale with **Generate Notes from Key** are staccato while it's on; recorded notes aren't. A staccato note has a dot centred on its notehead, on the side away from the stem, in a space rather than on a line: a space past a note in a space, and past a note on a line, in the space beyond the one its notehead reaches into. Notes starting on the same beat share one stem, so they're all staccato or none are, with one dot, by the notehead at the end away from the stem; a note added to them makes them staccato or not, as it is. Staccato notes are played short and detached: for half their written length, as notation programs play them, with silence for the rest. A note tied to the next is held on into it, so it's the last of tied notes that says whether they're let go early. With the sustain pedal down, staccato notes go on sounding until it comes up, as on a piano. Staccato is saved with the score, and Clone copies it; scores saved before there was staccato open with plain notes.

On a staff with a chord's notes, clicking works the same way on the chord:
- **Removing:** clicking one of the chord's notes takes it out of the chord. One in another octave from the chord's own, such as an arpeggio's octave at the top or a note moved there with Edit, takes out the chord's note of the same name.
- **Random chords:** a Random chord's notes are taken out one at a time instead, and only the note under the pointer turns red. Clicking it, or erasing it, takes the chord out of the measure: its other notes, on both staves, stay exactly where and as long as they were, as notes of their own, as if they'd been clicked in one by one, and the measure's button says **Add Chord** again. Undo puts the chord back. Block, rolled and arpeggio chords keep their chords, with the note taken out of them.
- **Adding:** clicking anywhere else on the staff adds that note to the chord, and the chord's other notes stay.
- **Both staves:** this works on the chord's own staff and on its alternate staff.
- **Renaming:** a chord changed this way is renamed for its new notes, as with notes set on the piano.
- **Resetting:** changing the chord in the chord window gives the alternate staff the chord's own notes back.
- **Empty chord:** taking out all the chord's notes frees the staff for quarter notes again.

### Dynamics

Eight buttons beside the note lengths, showing **ppp**, **pp**, **p**, **mp**, **mf**, **f**, **ff** and **fff**, choose a dynamic for clicks to mark instead of adding notes. While one is chosen, no note length shows as chosen, and clicking either instrument's staves marks the dynamic between its staves, at the nearest beat, or halfway between beats where a note starts there on either staff. A faint copy shows where it will go. Clicking a different dynamic's mark replaces it, and clicking the same one's takes it out. Clicking the chosen dynamic's button again, or any note length, goes back to adding notes.

Each instrument has its own dynamics. A dynamic sets how hard the instrument's notes are played, as MIDI velocity, from where it's marked until the next one, across barlines: ppp 20, pp 35, p 51, mp 66, mf 81, f 96, ff 112 and fff 127. Notes before the first dynamic play at 102, as every note did before there were dynamics. Playback that starts part way through the score uses the dynamic marked before that point. A tied note keeps the velocity it started with. Clone copies dynamics with the measures, and Copy Chords leaves the other instrument's dynamics as they are. A dynamic on a beat a shorter time signature leaves out is kept but doesn't show or play, as with notes. Dynamics are saved with the score.

**Crescendos and decrescendos.** After the dynamics are a crescendo button (<) and a decrescendo button (>). With one chosen, drag along an instrument's staves to mark it: it's drawn between the staves as a hairpin, a wedge opening out for a crescendo or closing for a decrescendo, and stretches with the mouse as you drag, from the beat where the mouse went down to the beat nearest where it's let go (or a barline, or halfway through a beat where a note starts). A click without dragging marks one a beat long. Drag the end of a hairpin to stretch or shrink it; the pointer becomes a left-right arrow over it. Clicking a hairpin takes it out. Hairpins can run across barlines, but not past the end of the score, and one marked over others takes their place. A hairpin starts after a dynamic marked where it starts, and ends before one where it ends.

Through a hairpin, each beat takes the velocity 10% of the way from where it is to the loudest, 127, for a crescendo, or to the softest, 20, for a decrescendo, so a crescendo from pp grows faster than one from ff: from pp (35) a beat later it's 44, and from ff (112) 114. Notes part way through a beat are part of the way there. After the hairpin, the velocity stays where it left it, until the next dynamic. A dynamic in the middle of a hairpin sets the velocity, and the hairpin carries on from there. Hairpins are saved with the score, and Clone copies them with the measures.

### Pedal

The pedal button, after the hairpins, marks the sustain pedal. With it chosen, click on or under an instrument's lower staff: the first click puts the pedal down there, the next lifts it, and they go on alternating, down and up. The marks are a line under the lower staff, below the numerals, with a tick up where the pedal goes down and another where it comes up. Clicking between where the pedal goes down and comes up lifts it and puts it straight down again there, so the line breaks in two with a little gap, a tick up either side of it. Clicking where it comes up takes that out, so it stays down through where the next goes down, if there is one, to where that comes up, or to the end of the score; the tick turns red to show a click will do that. Clicking where it's lifted and put straight down again takes out both, joining the two into one. Clicks go to the nearest beat, or a 32nd note where a note starts. A click before the pedal that's down lifts it there instead, and marking one over others takes their place. One put down and not lifted is held to the next pedal mark, or the end of the score.

While the pedal's down, every note of that instrument that's still sounding as it goes down, or ends before it comes up, is held until it comes up, so they ring on together; a note played again while it's held is stopped just before it's struck again. The instrument hears the pedal too, as the MIDI sustain pedal (controller 64), all the way down and up again where it's marked, so a piano plugin can add its own pedal resonance; where it's lifted and put straight down again, it comes up all the way and goes down again a twentieth of a second later, so the instrument hears the lift; the notes ending there stop as it comes up and the notes starting there come after. Playing from part way through the score with the pedal already down puts it down from the start, and stopping lets it up. Each instrument has its own pedal. The eraser takes out a pedal mark, anywhere along it. Pedal marks are saved with the score, Clone copies them, and they're undone like any other change.

### Repeats

After the pedal, in a group of their own, are the **start repeat** (a thick line, a thin one and two dots, ‖:) and **end repeat** (:‖) buttons. With one chosen, click a measure on any instrument's staves to put that repeat sign at its start or its end; a faint sign shows where it'll go before you click. Clicking a measure that has one takes it out, and it's red under the pointer to show it will. Repeat signs are the whole score's, so a sign marked on one instrument's staves is on every instrument's at once, and the measures make room for them, keeping every instrument's beats lined up. An end repeat takes the place of the measure's barline, and one at the end of the score of the final barline; where an end repeat meets a start repeat, the two share their thick line.

The score plays as written music does. The first time the music reaches an end repeat, it goes back to the start repeat before it, and plays from there again; the second time, it goes on past it. So with signs around the second of three measures, A, B, C plays as A, B, B, C. An end repeat with no start repeat before it goes back to the beginning, so A, B :‖ C plays as A, B, A, B, C; a later end repeat with no start repeat of its own goes back to just after the end repeat before it. All the instruments, the metronome and the pedal follow the repeats, and so do dynamics and hairpins, which carry on from wherever the music's come from.

While it plays, the staves' highlighted beat and the ruler's marker show where it is in the measures as written, going back with the music. Choosing a beat on the ruler plays from the first time that beat comes. Adding or taking out repeat signs while it plays carries on from the same place in the score, the same time through. Recording follows the repeats too: what's played the second time through a repeated passage goes into its measures, alongside what was played the first time, and a key held as the music goes back for a repeat ends at the end repeat. Repeat signs are saved with the score, copied by **Clone**, taken away with the last measure, and undone like any other change; the eraser takes them out too.

### First and second endings

The last button in the repeat group, two brackets numbered 1 and 2, adds first and second endings. With it chosen, click the measure the first ending starts at:

- Before an end repeat, the first ending runs from the measure clicked to the end repeat.
- With no end repeat after it, the first ending runs to the end of the score, and an end repeat is added there.

A bracket numbered **1.** goes over the first ending, closed at its end, and a faint one shows where it'll go before you click, with the end repeat it would add. The second ending is added just after it: as many new measures, in every instrument, so the staves stay lined up, with the first ending's music copied into them, notes, chords, dynamics, hairpins and pedal marks and all, to change as you like. Its bracket, numbered **2.**, is left open at its end, as the music goes on. The brackets go over the treble staff, above the measure numbers, and the chord names and buttons move up to make room for them.

The first time through, the music plays the first ending and goes back at its end repeat; the second time, it skips the first ending and plays the second, and goes on from there. So A, B, C :‖ D, with the first ending over B and C, plays as A, B, C, A, B′, C′, D, where B′ and C′ are the second ending. The metronome, pedal, playback marker and recording follow it, as they do repeats.

Clicking a measure in either ending, with the endings chosen, takes out both brackets, leaving their measures, their music and the end repeat as they are, and the eraser does the same anywhere along a bracket. Endings can't overlap. They're saved with the score, copied by **Clone**, and undone in one go, measures and all.

### Eraser

The eraser button, floating over the top right of the staves, always in view and apart from the note lengths, dynamics, hairpins and pedal, turns the eraser on, and the pointer becomes an eraser. Hold the mouse button down and move over the staves, and whatever the eraser passes over is taken out: notes, by their noteheads; a chord's notes, which come out of the chord, as clicking them does; ties, letting go of the notes they join; dynamics; hairpins, anywhere along them; pedal marks; repeat signs; and endings' brackets. It catches everything along the way, however quickly it's moved. A click without moving takes out what's under the pointer. Before you press, whatever the eraser is over is shown in red. It works on either instrument's staves, and never adds anything.

Everything one stroke takes out is undone in one go. Clicking the eraser button again, or choosing a note length, dynamic or hairpin, goes back to adding those. Chord names and numerals aren't erased; take a chord out with Remove Chord in its window, or by erasing its notes. Turning the eraser on turns Edit off.

### Editing notes

The **Edit** button, just left of the eraser, turns editing on, and is blue while it's on. Clicking it again turns it off. While it's on, a note under the pointer is blue and the pointer becomes a hand:

- **Drag** a note to move it. While you drag, faint notes show where it will go, as they do where a click would add one, and it goes to the line or space and beat where you let go, on either staff, snapping to the nearest step of its length without its dot, as clicks add notes, or to where a note already starts. It keeps its length, and staccato if it is, and if it now runs over a barline, it fills its measure and is tied over to the rest of it in the next, the last of the tied notes staccato; what would run past the end of the score is left off. Moved along its own line or space, it keeps its sharp or flat; moved to another, it takes the key signature's. Notes it lands on are treated as when a note is added there: one starting at the same point joins it in a chord, taking its length. It won't move into a measure whose chord has that staff.
- **Tied notes move together.** The pointer over a tied note highlights the notes tied to it, either way, and dragging any of them moves them all as far, still tied, with faint ties between the faint notes as you drag. Where they all still fit in their measures, they're written as they were; where one would cross a barline, they're written again from where the first lands, as one note as long as all of them, tied over the barlines.
- **Click** a note, without dragging, for a menu of note lengths, each shown with its note, and its own length ticked. Lengths too long for the rest of the measure are greyed out. Choosing one changes the note's length, and the notes starting with it change too, as notes starting together share a length. **Staccato**, under them, with the staccato dot, is ticked if the note is staccato; choosing it makes the note and the notes starting with it staccato, or plain again. **Delete**, at the bottom, takes the note out.
- **A chord's notes:** an arpeggio's or Random chord's notes, on the chord's own staff, are blue under the pointer too, and dragging one moves it to another of the chord's notes, in any octave, even if that breaks up the arpeggio: whichever is nearest the line or space where you let go, as high or low as a click reaches, on the note's own beat. A faint note shows which while you drag. It can't go to a note outside the chord: a C major chord's notes go only to Cs, Es and Gs. The other notes stay where they are, a Random chord keeps its order, and the moved notes are played as they're written. Clicking one without dragging takes it out, as it does without Edit (see [Notes](#notes)). The notes stay where they were moved until something changes what the chord writes: choosing anything in its chord window, including its chord type, even the one it is already, which puts them back where that type has them; adding a note to the chord or taking one out; another key; or a time signature or note length that gives it a different number of notes.

Anywhere there isn't a note, clicks and drags do what they do anyway: they add notes of the length chosen, or mark the dynamic, hairpin or pedal chosen, so you can edit and add without switching back and forth. Choosing a note length, dynamic, hairpin or pedal leaves Edit on. A block or rolled chord's notes, and the alternate staff's, aren't edited this way; clicking one takes it out of the chord, as before. Each move or change is undone in one go, tied notes and all.

### Selecting measures

The **Select** button, between Edit and the eraser, turns selecting measures on, and is blue while it's on. While it's on, clicks and drags on the staves select whole measures instead of adding notes, and no note length shows as chosen:

- **Drag** across the staves of any instrument to select every measure from the one you pressed on to the one the mouse is over, or the first or last, past either end of the score. A click selects just the measure clicked, and a click past the last measure selects none. The measures are the page's, so they're selected in every instrument, shown as one blue box over each instrument's staves.
- Once you let go, **Delete** and **Move** show over the selection, halfway down what's in view, staying over it as the staves scroll.
- **Delete** takes the selected measures out of every instrument, with everything in them (notes, chords, dynamics, hairpins, pedal marks and repeat signs), and the measures after them move up to where the selection started.
- **Move** turns moving on, and is blue while it's on. Then drag the selection along: a blue line at the barline nearest the mouse shows where the measures will go, between two others, before the first, or after the last, and letting go puts them there, in every instrument, with everything in them. On one of their own barlines, there's no line, and they stay where they are. They stay selected where they go, with Move still on, to move again; dragging from anywhere else selects measures again, and turns Move off. Near either edge of what's in view, the staves scroll along, so they can go further.
- **Pushing them on:** with no measures after the selection, dragging it on to the right puts empty measures in before it as you go, one for each empty measure's width, in every instrument, and the selection moves on with the mouse, still selected. Dragging back takes out the empty measures the drag put in, and dragging on back among the other measures moves the selection between them, as before. Held at the right edge of what's in view, the staves keep scrolling, and more measures go in. Measures with others after them, dragged past the end, go to the end; drag them again from there to push them on.
- With every measure selected, Delete is greyed out, as a page keeps at least one measure, but Move isn't: they can be pushed on, with empty measures before them.
- Ties between notes that don't follow one another any more let go, and a first or second ending left without its other half, or a first ending without its end repeat, is taken out, leaving its measures. A chord window that's open closes.
- Each Delete or move is undone in one go, and so is everything one drag does, however many measures it puts in. Esc, Undo, going to another page, or the measures going, lets go of the selection.
- Clicking Select again goes back to adding notes. Choosing a note length, dynamic, hairpin, pedal, repeat sign, Edit or the eraser turns Select off, and turning Select on turns Edit and the eraser off.

### Chords

Above each measure of each instrument is an **Add Chord** button, or **Edit Chord** if the measure has a chord. It makes the instrument active and opens the chord window for that measure, and outlines the measure while the window is open. Making another instrument active closes it, and so does deleting it or an instrument before it. For a chord that's already there, the window starts with its settings.

Everything you change in the window goes straight into the measure, and the staff updates as you go. **Remove Chord** empties the measure, leaving the window open for a new chord. **Done**, Return, Esc or the window's close button closes it. Clicking another measure's button moves the window on to that measure. The window floats over the main window, so the staff and piano can still be used while it's open, and changes made to the chord on the staff show in the window too.

The chord window has the Chord Progression Builder's settings for one chord:

1. **♭**, the checkbox to the right of the added note, lowers the chord's root a half step, so VII becomes ♭VII. **♯**, beside it, raises the root a half step instead, as for a minor key's leading-tone chord: in A minor, ♯vii° is G♯ diminished. Ticking one unticks the other. In a minor key, a chord set on the piano whose root is the raised 6th or 7th is named as raised, so G♯ B D in A minor is ♯vii°; any other root outside the scale is a lowered degree.
2. **Major / Minor** chooses the triad, and **+ / °** makes a major chord augmented or a minor chord diminished. **sus2** and **sus4**, under the added note, suspend the chord: **sus2** puts its 2nd in place of its 3rd (C D G for I in C) and **sus4** its 4th (C F G), so it's neither major nor minor, and + / ° can't be chosen. Each is a toggle: clicking one turns it on, and the other off, and clicking it again turns it off, giving the chord its 3rd again, as choosing Major or Minor does. A new chord has neither on. A suspended chord can have a 7th or 9th added, a minor chord's becoming a dominant 7th or 9th: V7sus4 in C is G C D F. It's named Isus4, Csus4, G7sus4 and so on, with its numeral written in capitals, and sus2 or sus4 after the numeral's figures on the staff. Notes set on the piano, or clicked into a chord on the staff, that make one, such as C F G, are taken as one.
3. **Numeral** is the scale step. Each option names the chord in the current key, e.g. "vi (Am)". Choose "—" for no chord.
4. **Added note** makes a 7th or 9th chord. It can't be on with + / °; choosing one clears the other.
5. **Position** is root position or the 1st, 2nd or 3rd inversion. The 3rd is only for 7th and 9th chords.
6. **Octave** moves the chord down or up an octave.
7. **Treble clef / Bass clef** is the staff the chord is written on. Its lowest note falls in C4–B4 on the treble staff and E2–D3 on the bass.
8. **Chord type**, in the menu at the top right of the window, beside the instrument and measure:
   - **Block chord**: a chord held for its note length, the whole measure to start with, written at its start so it lines up with a quarter note on the first beat of the other staff. With a shorter note length it's played again until the measure's full, e.g. on every beat in quarter notes, with a rest for anything left over.
   - **Arpeggio (asc)**: single notes going up through the chord to the first note an octave higher, unless the chord already reaches that far, and back down, over and over until the measure's full, every note the chord's note length: in eighths in 4/4, C E G C' G E C E. Notes are beamed like notes clicked in (four eighths in a row together).
   - **Arpeggio (desc)**: back and forth between the same notes, turning at the C at the bottom and the C an octave up, but starting on the chord's highest note and going down first, so it carries on from an arpeggio going up in the measure before: G E C E G C' G E. A chord that reaches past the octave, such as a 9th chord, turns at its own top and bottom notes instead.
   - **Random**: the notes in a random order, a note of the note length after another, without playing any note twice in a row, and every note at least once if there's room. Choosing **Random** in the menu picks a new random order every time, even when the chord's Random already.
   - An arpeggio's or Random chord's notes can be moved to others of its notes, in any octave, with Edit (see [Editing notes](#editing-notes)). Choosing a chord type, even the one it is already, puts them back where that type writes them.
   - **Rolled chord**: a block chord with the wavy roll sign.
9. **Note length**, under the clef buttons, is how long the chord's notes are: **Whole measure** (a whole note in 4/4, a dotted half in 3/4 and a half in 2/4), half, quarter, eighth, 16th or 32nd notes, but not dotted ones; lengths longer than the measure can't be chosen. A block or rolled chord starts out lasting the measure; arpeggios and Random chords start out in quarter notes, or eighths or 16ths if there are too many notes for quarters to fit, and follow the chord's notes as they change until a length is chosen. **Two measures**, at the top of the list, writes a block or rolled chord in two measures, the one it's in and the next, lasting each of them, tied over from the first to the second, so it's played once and held for both; a rolled chord is rolled as it starts and not again. The next measure is added if it's the last. It's one chord in both, as chords tied over measures are (see Measures per chord, under [Score](#score)), and choosing a length of a measure or less puts it back in one measure. Arpeggios and Random chords, played a note at a time, can't have it. A chord starts out as it did before, a block chord lasting the measure.
10. **Rhythm**, under Note length, writes the chord's notes in a saved rhythm (see Save Rhythm, under [Score](#score)) in place of the note length, which is greyed out while one's chosen; **No rhythm** goes back to it. The rhythm is kept in the chord, so the score has it even if it's deleted from the ones saved, and a chord's own rhythm that isn't saved is listed too, as "(this chord's)".
    - A **block chord** is written all together for each of the rhythm's notes, as long as it, with the rhythm's rests between them, and staccato where it was; a **rolled chord** too, rolled each time it starts afresh.
    - An **arpeggio** goes a note at a time, the next of its notes, up and down, for each of the rhythm's notes, so a rhythm of four eighths and a half note gives C E G C' G; a **Random chord** goes in its random order, with a note in it for each.
    - A note the rhythm ties to the one before it is the same note held on: a block chord's notes all held on, and an arpeggio's single note, so the next note of the arpeggio goes on the note after. Ties are drawn, and played as one note held for both.
    - A rhythm on both staves, with the alternate staff showing something, writes the chord in its own staff's notes and the alternate staff's notes in the other staff's, so the two stay in step as they were saved. With a rhythm on one staff, the chord goes in its notes on either staff, and the alternate staff holds its notes for the measure, as it does without one.
    - A rhythm longer than a measure takes as many measures as it needs, one after another, with the chord in each of them, adding measures at the end if they're needed; a rhythm saved in another time signature runs on over the barlines. What was in those measures goes. Each of them shows the chord, with its own Edit Chord button, which opens the window for the measure the chord starts in. Changing the chord there, or clicking a note into it or out of it on the staff, changes it in all its measures; choosing a rhythm with fewer measures, or No rhythm, leaves the measures it no longer takes empty; and Remove Chord takes it out of all of them. A Random chord's new order goes in all of them.

Each chord keeps its own clef, chord type, note length and rhythm. A new chord starts with the ones chosen last. A chord replaces any quarter notes on its staff.

Under the settings, the window lists the chord's name and position, its notes from low to high, and whether they're in the key.

**On the piano:** while the chord window is open, clicking a key adds its note to the window's chord, or takes it out. The chord's notes are marked on the keys. The notes are named as a note, an interval, or a chord (including sus2, sus4, ø7, °7 and m(maj7)), and spelled to suit it, e.g. A♭ rather than G♯ in B°7. When the window can describe the notes, it fills in to match. Notes set on the piano keep their pitches if you change the key.

### What the staff shows

- Chord names above each chord, e.g. Am, G7, B♭maj9, B°, E+, and slash chords for inversions such as G/B.
- Roman numerals below, with their figures: I⁶, I⁶₄, V⁷, V⁶₅, V⁴₃, V⁴₂, V⁹, and an M before a major 7th's figures (I^M7).
- Accidentals only where a note differs from the key signature or from an earlier accidental in the measure.

### Playing

Every note is held for exactly as long as it's written on the staff, whatever wrote it: a quarter note for a beat, a half note for two, a whole note for four, a dotted quarter for a beat and a half, an eighth for half of one, a dotted eighth for three quarters of one, a 16th for a quarter of one, a 32nd for an eighth of one, with the next note starting as it ends. Block chords sound together and rolled chords bottom to top in quick succession; both are written as notes as long as the measure (a whole note in 4/4, a dotted half in 3/4), so that's how long they're held. Arpeggio and Random notes come one per written note, each for its own length. Alternate-staff notes start on the downbeat and are held for their length too. The only notes cut shorter are staccato notes, which sound for half their length (see [Notes](#notes)), and one whose pitch starts again while it's still sounding, as a key can't be pressed twice at once.

Click or drag across the piano keys to play the active instrument yourself. Once the piano has been clicked, the computer keyboard plays it too: A is middle C, and the row from A to L (with W, E, T, Y, U, O and P for the black keys) plays upwards from there.

### Recording

While recording, each key played on the piano, the computer keyboard or a MIDI controller is written into the active instrument's staves as it's let go of, as a note as long as it was held, where the score had got to when it went down. Keys from middle C up go on the treble staff, and those below on the bass.

- Notes start and end on the nearest 16th note, so a note is at least a 16th long. Within a measure a note is written as the length nearest to how long it was held (halfway between two, the shorter), as long as that doesn't run into a note already on the staff after it. A note held across a barline fills its measure to the barline and is tied over it, to a whole note for each measure it's held right through, and in the measure where it's let go of, the length nearest to the rest of it.
- Keys pressed together, within a 16th, make a chord, which takes the length of whichever is let go of last. A staff has one line of notes, so a key still held when another starts on its staff ends there.
- The notes are spelled in the key, B♭ rather than A♯ in F major, say. They take the place of what they're written over, as notes added by clicking do, and a measure whose chord has the staff is left alone.
- Keys still held when recording stops end there, or at the end of the score when it plays to the end. Recording stops when the score does.
- With **Loop** on, recording goes on through each time round. What was recorded is heard the next time through, and more can be played along with it. A key held as the loop goes back to the beginning lasts to the end of the score.
- Recording the whole piece, with **Piece**, what's played on a page goes on that page, whichever page you're looking at. A key held as the music goes on to the next page ends at the end of its page.
- Everything one recording adds is undone in one go. Choosing another instrument while recording records the next keys into that one.

### MIDI controllers

Plug a MIDI controller into a USB port and it plays the active instrument like the on-screen piano, whose keys go down as you play. It's used as soon as it's plugged in, even with the app already running. The keys play at the velocity you strike them, and the sustain pedal, pitch bend, mod wheel and other controls go through to the plugin too. While the chord window is open, a key adds its note to the window's chord or takes it out, as clicking the piano does.

The **MIDI** menu lists the MIDI inputs, ticked when they're on. Choose one to turn it off or on; one that's off stays off the next time the app starts.
